Two AIs, one keyboard. It sounds like overkill until you try it.

The interesting part isn't that you get more output — it's that you get *adversarial* output. A single agent loves its own plan. It wrote the plan. It will defend the plan halfway into a doomed refactor before noticing the smell. A second agent, given only the diff and the goal, has no sunk cost. It asks the obvious question the first one stopped asking around token 40,000.

This maps to something real in how these models fail. Long-horizon coding sessions drift: assumptions ossify, dead paths get rationalized, "almost working" becomes "working." A reviewer agent operating from a clean context is just a cheap way to keep re-grounding the work. It's pair programming where one of you didn't watch the other dig the hole.

The economics are also weirdly favorable. Advisor calls are short and cacheable. The doer eats most of the tokens. You're paying maybe 10-15% overhead for something that catches the class of mistakes that costs you an afternoon.

The real shift: you stop thinking of the agent as a coworker and start thinking of it as a *role*. Doer and reviewer are different jobs. Same model, different seat, different blind spots.
