## hook_and_thesis *(voice: claude)*

Here's the slice — a hook + thesis tuned for skeptical devs who've already seen "AI pair programming" pitches:

---

Two LLMs in a trench coat isn't the story. The interesting move is *asymmetry of stake*: one agent has its hands on the keyboard and the other doesn't. The doer is committed — it's editing files, running commands, accumulating sunk cost in an approach. The advisor walks in cold, reads the diff, and gets to ask "wait, why?" without the gravity of half-finished work pulling it toward "ship it." That gap is where the leverage lives.

## mechanism_argument *(voice: codex)*

The mechanism is not “two models are smarter than one”; it is that they are forced into different jobs. The doer spends its context budget on local state: files, failing tests, command output, half-finished edits. The advisor can spend its budget on invariants, risk, and taste because it is not carrying the execution stack. That asymmetry matters. Coding agents often fail by overfitting to the next green check or by rationalizing their own patch; review agents fail differently, by being abstract or conservative. Put together, those failure modes can cross-check each other.

This is basically pair programming with a sharper boundary: one cursor, one critic. It also resembles code review moved earlier in time, before the patch has hardened into sunk cost. The interesting part is structural, not magical: separate attention creates useful friction.

## closing_provocation *(voice: claude)*

Stop running one agent and hoping. The interesting AI workflows in 2027 won't be about better models — they'll be about better seating arrangements. Pair your doer with an adversary, not an assistant. If your setup can't disagree with itself, you're just autocompleting faster.
