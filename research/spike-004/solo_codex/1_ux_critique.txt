Start by asking what decisions users actually need to make.

A settings page with 12 toggles is often overwhelming because every option looks equally important. I’d restructure it around intent:

1. **Group related toggles**
   Use clear sections like `Notifications`, `Privacy`, `Display`, `Advanced`, etc. Don’t show 12 unrelated switches in one flat list.

2. **Set strong defaults**
   If most users should leave something alone, make the default good and avoid forcing them to think about it.

3. **Prioritize common controls**
   Put the 3-5 settings users most often change at the top. Move rare or risky options into an `Advanced` section.

4. **Use plain-language labels**
   Avoid technical labels like “Enable async sync.” Use outcome-based copy like “Sync changes in the background.”

5. **Explain consequences only where needed**
   Short helper text is useful for ambiguous or high-impact toggles, but don’t add paragraphs under every switch.

6. **Consider presets**
   If the toggles often form common combinations, offer modes like `Recommended`, `Strict privacy`, or `Minimal notifications`, then let users customize.

The goal is not necessarily to reduce the number from 12. The goal is to reduce the number of decisions users feel they must understand at once.
