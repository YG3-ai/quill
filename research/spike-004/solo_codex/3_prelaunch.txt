Tomorrow is **Sunday, May 17, 2026 at noon**. For a payments launch, I’d check these before you ship:

1. **Rollback path**
   Can you disable the new integration without redeploying? Feature flag, routing switch, or config-only rollback should be ready.

2. **Idempotency**
   Retries must not double-charge. Confirm idempotency keys are used consistently for payment creation, webhook handling, and order fulfillment.

3. **Webhook failure handling**
   Test duplicate, delayed, out-of-order, and missing webhooks. Your system should reconcile from the provider API, not rely only on webhooks.

4. **Money correctness**
   Verify currency, rounding, tax, discounts, partial refunds, full refunds, failed captures, authorization expiry, and zero/edge-value orders.

5. **Observability**
   Have dashboards and alerts for payment success rate, provider errors, webhook lag/failures, duplicate attempts, refund failures, and order/payment mismatches.

6. **Customer support path**
   Support needs clear answers for: charged but no order, order created but payment pending, duplicate charge, refund timing, bank decline, and provider outage.

7. **Provider outage behavior**
   Decide what users see if the provider is degraded. Avoid ambiguous states like “order confirmed” when payment status is unknown.

8. **Reconciliation**
   Run a post-launch job/report comparing internal orders against provider charges, captures, refunds, and disputes.

9. **Security/compliance**
   Confirm you are not storing raw card data, logs do not contain sensitive payment fields, secrets are rotated/stored correctly, and webhook signatures are verified.

10. **Launch staffing**
   Have an engineer, support contact, and decision-maker available for the first few hours after noon. Define who can call rollback.

The biggest misses are usually not the happy-path payment request. They are **duplicate retries, webhook edge cases, bad user-facing pending states, and no fast rollback**.
