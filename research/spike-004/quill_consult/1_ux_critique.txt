Treat this less as a layout problem and more as a product decision problem.

A settings page with 12 toggles often feels overwhelming because every option is presented as equally important. Start by auditing the toggles:

1. **Remove toggles that should be product defaults**
   Ask: “Do users genuinely need different behavior here, or did we add this because the team could not decide?”
   If most users should use one behavior, make that the default and remove the toggle.

2. **Group the remaining toggles by user goal**
   Avoid a flat list. Use sections like:
   - Notifications
   - Privacy
   - Display
   - Automation
   - Advanced

3. **Make defaults opinionated**
   Users should not feel like they must configure the product before it works well. Good defaults reduce anxiety.

4. **Hide rarely used or risky options**
   Put power-user controls under “Advanced” or behind contextual flows. Do not make every user scan settings they will never touch.

5. **Rewrite labels around outcomes**
   Bad: `Enable enhanced mode`
   Better: `Automatically archive completed projects`

6. **Prioritize the top 3-5 settings**
   If only a few settings are commonly changed, visually promote those and demote the rest.

A good target is not “12 toggles but prettier.” It is probably “5 visible decisions, strong defaults, and the rest either removed, grouped, or moved into advanced/contextual places.”
