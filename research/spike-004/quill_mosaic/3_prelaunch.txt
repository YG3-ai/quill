## operational_readiness *(voice: claude)*

Here's my read on what usually gets missed in the human/process layer for a T-minus-24h payments launch:

**The on-call setup is probably nominal-but-fragile.** A primary engineer is named, sure, but check: is there a *named secondary* who is awake during the launch window and has actually been added to PagerDuty (not just "Slack me")? For a noon launch, the risk window extends through end-of-day US/EU traffic — so you need explicit hand-off coverage, not just the launch hour. And critically: has the pager been *tested* in the last 48 hours? I'd also ask whether the on-call rotation includes someone with database/ledger access, not just app-tier engineers — a reconciliation drift incident at 2pm needs different hands than a webhook 500 storm. War-room channel pre-created, with exec/finance/support/legal liaisons already invited rather than scrambled-for at incident time.

**The runbook is where I'd push hardest.** "Payments are failing right now" is too coarse — the real runbook needs three branches: (1) auth-rate cliff (provider outage vs. our key/config issue — kill-switch to fall back or pause checkout, with a single feature flag the on-call can flip without a deploy), (2) webhooks delayed or dropped (do we replay from provider? is our idempotency key handling proven against duplicate-delivery?), (3) ledger drift detected (do we *stop* settlement or let it accumulate while we investigate?). Each branch needs the exact dashboard URL, the exact command/flag, and the decision threshold for paging finance. Also: who has authority to pause checkout? If that's a VP-only call, document it now — incidents are not the time to discover the approval chain.

**Dashboards/alerts — the gap I see most often is *thresholds set on launch-day traffic, not steady-state.*** P95 latency at 1 req/sec is meaningless; you need either synthetic traffic baseline or a "ramp-aware" alert that doesn't fire on the first 20 transactions. Webhook lag alert should be on *p99 age of unacked events*, not average. Reconciliation drift needs to alert on absolute dollar variance, not just count — one missed $10k transaction matters more than ten missed $1 ones. Confirm alerts route to a channel that's actually watched at noon (not just email).

**The cross-functional sign-offs are where calendars lie.** Verify *in writing* (Slack message, email, ticket) that you have: finance sign-off on the specific ledger reconciliation cadence and the chart-of-accounts mapping; support team has the *exact wording* of the top 5 user-facing error messages and the refund SOP including who approves refunds >$X; legal has confirmed the ToS update is *live* (not "ready to publish") and that regional restrictions are enforced in code, not just policy; tax has confirmed the integration's tax calculation matches what's been agreed for each jurisdiction. The "we talked about it last week" sign-off evaporates during an incident.

**Customer-facing comms — pre-write the three messages now, don't draft them at 12:15pm.** (1) "We're investigating an issue with checkout" (status page banner + in-app), (2) "Checkout is temporarily paused, your cart is saved" (the graceful pause), (3) "If you were charged but didn't get a confirmation, here's what to do" (the double-charge / orphan-charge script for support). Get marketing/comms approval on the wording *today* so on-call isn't writing copy under pressure.

**One question back to you:** is there a no-go decision point defined? Something like "if auth success rate is below X% in the first 30 minutes, we pause and re-evaluate" — with a named decision-maker. Launches without a pre-committed rollback criterion tend to limp forward past the point where rolling back was cheap.
